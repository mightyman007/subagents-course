Welcome. This is a hands-on course about using AI coding subagents — child sessions you dispatch to do work while your main conversation stays clean. It covers OpenCode V2 and Claude Code side by side.

One thing before you start: **everything on this site runs in your browser.** There is no account, no server, and nothing to configure. Read a chapter, take its quiz, flip its deck. That's the whole loop.

## How a chapter is built

Every chapter follows the same shape:

1. **Objectives** — a short list at the top of what you should be able to *do* after reading. Use them as a checklist when you finish.
2. **The lesson** — the main body, with diagrams, illustrations, and videos where they help.
3. **A quiz** — a handful of questions with instant per-question feedback and an explanation for every answer, right or wrong.
4. **A flashcard deck** — the chapter's key facts as flip cards, for spaced repetition over the following days.

Work through chapters **in order** — each one assumes the vocabulary of the ones before it. The [course index](/learn) lists them in sequence and shows your per-chapter state.

## Quizzes

Each quiz question is submitted individually: pick an answer, hit Submit, and you immediately see whether you were right, the correct answer, and *why* — every question in this course has a written explanation, so a wrong answer teaches you something instead of just scoring against you.

Two behaviors worth knowing:

- **Retakes are free.** Take a quiz again at any time; your newest score replaces the old one.
- **Scoring 60% or higher marks the chapter as done** in your progress. You can keep retaking if you want a cleaner score, but 60% is the bar this course uses for "you absorbed this".

## Flashcards

Decks show one card at a time. Click a card (or press Enter/Space on it) to flip from front to back, then mark yourself honestly:

- **Got it** — you recalled it without hesitation.
- **Again** — you didn't. It stays in rotation next run.

The deck shuffles with a fixed seed so the page renders consistently; every restart reshuffles into a fresh order. Marks are stored per card, so coming back to a deck restores your earlier marks — each run still starts again at the first card.

## The final exam

After the chapters comes a final exam covering the whole course. Unlike chapter quizzes, the exam gives **no feedback while you answer** — no per-question right/wrong, just a single submit at the end, a score, and a pass/fail against the **80% pass mark**. You can retake it. Treat it as the honest check that the material stuck, not a hurdle.

## How your progress is stored

Your quiz scores, done chapters, card marks, and exam result are saved **in your browser's localStorage** under the key `subagents-course:progress:v1`. That means:

- Nothing ever leaves your machine — there is no server to send it to.
- Progress is per-browser and per-device. If you switch browsers or machines, you start fresh.
- Clearing this site's data in your browser resets everything to zero.

If progress ever looks wrong, a reset is always safe: it just deletes that one key.

## Downloads

Two kinds of files accompany the course:

- **Agent templates** — ready-to-copy agent files you can drop straight into your own setup: a read-only code reviewer for OpenCode V2 (a subagent-mode agent with edit and shell access denied) and the equivalent for Claude Code. You'll meet both in the chapters on configuring agents; the files themselves live in the downloads area so you can grab them without copy-typing.
- **An Anki deck** — every flashcard in this course, packaged as a real `.apkg` you can import into Anki for long-term review on your phone.

Grab them whenever you reach the relevant chapter — nothing in the course requires them to complete the reading.

## One request

Do the quizzes before peeking at explanations, and mark flashcards honestly. The whole course is built on the assumption that you want to *know* this material, not just click through it. Ready? Start with [What are subagents](/learn/01-what-are-subagents).
